# Premium Visual Design

## Outcome
Create a distinctive, coherent and usable visual system tied to business positioning, audience, content and available assets—not a generic "premium" aesthetic.

## Process
1. Read approved strategy, content architecture, brand assets, constraints and relevant reference cards.
2. Build a visual brief: personality, differentiation, emotional tone, content priority and design risks.
3. Inspect a focused selection of references. Extract principles (composition, hierarchy, type scale, image treatment, navigation, rhythm, interaction), not copied layouts/assets.
4. Choose a design concept and explain why it serves the brand. Explore meaningful alternatives when the direction is uncertain.
5. Define tokens: color roles and contrast, typography roles and scale, spacing, container/grid, radii, borders, shadows, motion cues and responsive breakpoints.
6. Define component anatomy and states: default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, error and success where relevant.
7. Design the hero and one representative complex section first; validate hierarchy, imagery, readability and responsive behavior before scaling.
8. Select image strategy: art direction, crop, aspect ratio, focal point, image rights, alt text and mobile crop. Do not use decorative placeholders as final client content.
9. Audit common generic patterns: repetitive card grids, arbitrary gradients, decorative pills, meaningless eyebrow labels, excessive glassmorphism, stock icons, fake dashboards, random serif/italic accents and repetitive reveal animation. These are not forbidden, but each use needs a reason.
10. Check consistency across pages and states. Ensure visual differentiation does not harm usability or accessibility.

## Review rubric
Distinctiveness; brand fit; hierarchy; typography; contrast; spacing; imagery; consistency; responsive composition; accessibility; feasibility; maintainability. Identify severity and concrete correction, not just taste.

## Deliverables
Creative direction, design tokens, reference decisions (selected/adapted/rejected), page/component specs, responsive guidance and open asset/content needs.

## Learning progression
Beginner: explain hierarchy, contrast, spacing and type. Intermediate: build tokenized systems. Advanced: evaluate visual systems, art direction and component behavior across responsive states.
